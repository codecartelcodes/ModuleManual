# Product / Solution Template

## 1. Product Overview
- **Core Product Statement:**  
  Describe what the product is in one sentence and the problem it solves.  
  *Example: A fully-automated product that helps teams build software faster in a non-frustrating way.*

- **What It Does:**  
  Explain the main job the product performs for the customer.  
  *Example: It removes friction from finding, trying, buying, implementing, deploying, and owning the solution.*

---

## 2. Customer Gains
- **Revenue Impact:**  
  Describe how the product helps customers make more money.  
  *Example: Increases conversion, improves output, and creates new usage opportunities.*

- **Cost Savings:**  
  Explain how the product reduces waste or operating expense.  
  *Example: Cuts manual work, lowers tooling overhead, and reduces rework.*

- **Time Savings:**  
  State how the product makes teams move faster.  
  *Example: Accelerates delivery, reduces cycle time, and removes delays.*

- **People / Team Impact:**  
  Describe how the product improves the experience of the team using it.  
  *Example: Less frustration, fewer handoffs, and more focus on high-value work.*

- **Competitive Advantage:**  
  Explain how the product helps customers outperform alternatives.  
  *Example: Ships faster, detects issues earlier, and improves execution quality.*

- **Reputation / Trust Impact:**  
  Describe how the product strengthens customer confidence and brand trust.  
  *Example: Improves reliability, compliance posture, and stakeholder confidence.*

---

## 3. Buyer Objections
- **Reasons Not to Buy:**  
  List the strongest objections, doubts, or blockers a customer may have.  
  *Example: Too complex, too slow, unclear ROI, hard to implement, or not different enough.*

- **Friction Points:**  
  Identify any step that makes adoption difficult.  
  *Example: Find, try, buy, implement, deploy, and own are all too hard.*

- **Switching Barriers:**  
  Explain what would prevent a customer from changing from their current solution.  
  *Example: Existing workflow lock-in, migration effort, and fear of disruption.*

---

## 4. Switching Triggers
- **Why Customers Switch:**  
  State what causes a customer to leave their current solution.  
  *Example: The current process is too slow, too manual, or too painful to maintain.*

- **Disruption Drivers:**  
  Describe the conditions that make your product meaningfully better.  
  *Example: The product is disruptive, defensible, and discontinuous enough to force reconsideration.*

- **Adoption Moment:**  
  Explain what makes the product feel like an obvious upgrade.  
  *Example: A simpler workflow, clearer ROI, or instant visible value.*

---

## 5. Product Architecture
- **Product Type:**  
  Define whether this is software, service, platform, workflow, or hybrid.  
  *Example: A fully-automated application security service with a software-first delivery model.*

- **Core Product Description:**  
  Summarize the main components or architecture if relevant.  
  *Example: Stateless analysis engine, lightweight CLI, and scalable backend pipeline.*

- **Operating Model:**  
  Explain how the product runs and supports the user.  
  *Example: Simple, sparse-resource friendly, scalable, and supervised where needed.*

---

## 6. Proof & Experience
- **Screenshots / UX Flow / Demo:**  
  Describe the visual or interactive proof that shows how the product works.  
  *Example: Include onboarding screens, scan flow, result dashboard, and demo link.*

- **Compliance / Trust Layer:**  
  Note any relevant trust signals, certifications, or compliance posture.  
  *Example: Integrates with compliance workflows such as Vanta-style reporting.*

- **Supported Environments:**  
  List the primary usage contexts or deployment modes.  
  *Example: SaaS, CI/CD, MCP, command line, and developer workflow integrations.*

---

## 7. Language & Scope
- **Supported Languages / Formats:**  
  Define the technical scope of the product.  
  *Example: Bash, JSON, Python, JavaScript, TypeScript, Go, Java, Rust, SQL, HTML, CSS, and other refactorable top-level-function languages.*

- **Use Case Expansion:**  
  Describe how the product creates multiple opportunities across different contexts.  
  *Example: One core capability can support SaaS, CI/CD, automation, and internal developer tooling.*

---

## 8. Product Summary Statement
- **Concise Summary:**  
  Write one paragraph that captures what the product is, why it matters, and why it wins.  
  *Example: Our product is a fully-automated system that helps teams build faster without friction. It reduces pain across discovery, adoption, deployment, and ownership while delivering gains in revenue, cost savings, time, team productivity, and competitive advantage.*

---

> **Prompt Use Note:**  
> Use this template to describe the product clearly before generating a business plan or pitch deck. Each section should contain 1–3 crisp sentences with emphasis on automation, ease of use, customer gains, switching triggers, and product differentiation.
